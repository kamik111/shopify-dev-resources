# Shopify Development Quote Checklist

You asked a freelancer or agency to price your Shopify build, and now you are holding a
number. This checklist helps you read that quote line by line, spot the gaps and red
flags, and compare two estimates fairly before you sign anything. It is scope-first and
platform-agnostic: the platform is the cheap, predictable part — the labor is where the
money and the risk live.

For the full breakdown with numbers — real budget scenarios, hourly-rate bands, and what
drives a quote up — see the source article:
**[Shopify Store Development Cost](https://shopify.ecom-store.pro/blog/shopify-store-development-cost/)**.

---

## 1. What a complete quote should include

A Shopify build breaks into six parts. A quote that names all of them is easier to trust
and far easier to compare. Confirm each one is either priced or explicitly marked "not
included."

- [ ] **Platform plan** — which Shopify plan the build assumes (this is your recurring floor, not a build cost)
- [ ] **Theme** — free (Horizon) vs. a one-time paid theme, and whether the theme price is separate
- [ ] **Design & development** — the actual labor; this is the biggest and most variable line
- [ ] **Apps** — any third-party subscriptions the build depends on (reviews, email, subscriptions, etc.)
- [ ] **Content & data** — product photography, copywriting, and catalog migration
- [ ] **Support & maintenance** — post-launch fixes and updates (retainer, ad-hoc hours, or none)
- [ ] Every line is itemized — no single lump sum standing in for all of the above
- [ ] The one-time build cost is stated separately from any recurring monthly cost
- [ ] The quote lists what is **out of scope** as clearly as what is in scope

---

## 2. Confirm the scope is written down

Vague briefs produce vague quotes, and vague quotes are where overruns hide. Make sure the
estimate is priced against a concrete, written scope — ideally one you wrote.

- [ ] A written scope exists (pages, features, integrations, data) before any price was quoted
- [ ] The number of unique page templates is defined (home, product, collection, landing, etc.)
- [ ] Every integration is named (ERP, POS, warehouse, subscriptions, tax, CRM)
- [ ] Data migration is scoped: products, variants, customers, orders, URLs, and redirects
- [ ] Revision rounds are capped and defined, so "one more change" is not open-ended
- [ ] Anything added later is handled as a priced change order, not an assumed favor

---

## 3. Sanity-check the numbers against published benchmarks

You cannot budget from an official Shopify figure — **Shopify publishes no Expert or
Partner rates anywhere on its site.** Instead, pressure-test the quote against the public
ranges below. A quote far outside them deserves a question, not blind trust.

- [ ] Ecommerce-type website **design** projects: Shopify cites **$500 to $10,000**
- [ ] Fully **custom** design and development: **$2,000 to $20,000 or more**, by complexity
- [ ] Freelancer hourly rate: roughly **$24–$49/hr** for ecommerce work (Clutch)
- [ ] Agency hourly rate: roughly **$50–$199/hr**, with project minimums of **$1,000 to $25,000+**
- [ ] Region can swing an hourly rate **4–6×** (about **$100–$149/hr** in the US vs. **under $25** in some regions)
- [ ] Context for the mean: Clutch reports an average ecommerce development project of **$51,943.13** — dragged up by enterprise builds, not the typical store
- [ ] A rate far above or below the regional norm has a written explanation

---

## 4. Red flags to slow down on

Any one of these is a reason to ask more questions before signing.

- [ ] **No line-item breakdown** — a lump sum hides what you are actually paying for
- [ ] **"We'll figure out scope as we go"** — open-ended hours are open-ended bills
- [ ] **Pressure to pay 100% upfront** — staged, milestone-tied payments protect you
- [ ] **No named contact or references** — you should reach a real person and a past client
- [ ] **Rates far outside regional norms** with no explanation — high or low both warrant a question
- [ ] **Build and retainer bundled together** — hides the true ongoing monthly commitment
- [ ] **No portfolio of live, shipped Shopify stores** — a pitch deck is not proof of delivery

---

## 5. Questions to ask before you sign

- [ ] Can I see live Shopify stores you have built, and speak to a past client?
- [ ] What is the one-time build cost vs. the ongoing support cost, as two separate lines?
- [ ] Which parts are custom code, and which reuse an existing theme or off-the-shelf app?
- [ ] What exactly is included in each milestone, and what does "done" mean for each?
- [ ] Which apps will the store depend on, and what do they cost per month?
- [ ] Who owns the code and the store admin after launch?
- [ ] What happens — and what does it cost — when I need changes after go-live?
- [ ] How are out-of-scope requests priced during the build?

## 6. Hidden and recurring first-year costs

The build is one-time; these keep billing every month after launch. Confirm the quote (or
your own budget) accounts for them — they are the line owners most often forget.

- [ ] **Shopify plan** — your recurring floor for the whole year
- [ ] **Domain** — from about **$9/year** through Shopify (auto-renews; WHOIS privacy included)
- [ ] **Apps** — subscriptions with no official average; priced by features, usage, or order volume
- [ ] **Payment processing** — a per-transaction percentage of sales, not a fixed monthly fee
- [ ] **Maintenance & support** — a retainer or ad-hoc hours at your builder's rate
- [ ] For a Plus-scale project, note that **Shopify Plus starts at $2,300/month** — a small part of a much larger enterprise total
- [ ] Frame of reference: Shopify puts essential recurring costs at **roughly $29/month at the low end, rising toward $10,000** as a store grows

---

## 7. How to compare two (or three) quotes fairly

- [ ] All quotes were priced against the **same written brief** — comparable inputs give comparable numbers
- [ ] You collected **at least three** estimates on that identical scope
- [ ] Each quote separates the **one-time build** from the **monthly retainer**
- [ ] You are comparing **year-one totals**, not just the headline build number
- [ ] You understand why the outlier — high or low — differs (who builds it, where they sit, how much is custom)
- [ ] The cheapest option can actually deliver the scope — a failed budget build costs more than a right-sized one
- [ ] Payments are tied to **milestones and acceptance criteria**, not calendar dates

---

## Before you sign

- [ ] The scope is locked in writing and both sides have signed off on it
- [ ] Payments are staged against deliverables, with clear acceptance criteria per milestone
- [ ] You have chosen the **smallest build that fits today's needs** — not a two-years-out wishlist
- [ ] Every custom feature is justified against the revenue or saved time it unlocks
